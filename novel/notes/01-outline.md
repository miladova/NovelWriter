# THE PRICE OF MERCY — Outline (private working notes)

Each chapter: title / date / POV / spine / closing turn. Target 5,000–6,000 words each.

---

## PART ONE — THE NET (May–August 1943)

**1. The Net** — Thu 20 May 1943 — Helena.
Bread, ration, Hoża 14; Marysia, the violin, the house; the double cordon at the market; the fight to stay together; the lorries; the bridge into Praga; Skaryszewska 8, the baths, the shaven heads, the lists; the Wehrmacht commission picking men for the workshops; Friedrich's face. Turn: he asks the child's age and Helena lies; both know what the number means.

**2. The Man in Grey** — Fri 21 May 1943 — Friedrich.
His office, his trade: billets, labour quotas, the odd dignity of a desk; Adler; the funeral of a boy from his old regiment; Erika's letter; the memory of Munich 1933, reconstructed honestly (including what he did not do); the file; Klara Seidler's card index; the arithmetic. Turn: he signs the child's name onto a different list, and knows he has just made himself a hostage.

**3. Hoża Street** — Sat 22–Sun 23 May 1943 — Helena.
Released from the camp after five days (hers is a "labour card" — for the Wehrmacht hospital laundry); the house without the child; the pawnshop (the violin nearly goes); the going price of an exemption; Wanda and the welfare office; Zofia; Rachel behind the coal; Tomek's first approach about the underground; the lie she has told Marysia all her life, rehearsed aloud in an empty room. Turn: the child is not on the transport list — because her name has been entered on another list, and Helena does not yet know which.

**4. The Saxon Garden** — Mon 24 May 1943 — Helena & Friedrich.
Ten years in one conversation under the lime trees by the fountain; the accusations; the signed paper; the half photograph; the truth that he has a daughter; the terms he lays down and the terms she refuses; the sentence that decides the book's middle: *the only road that keeps her in Warsaw is a road Helena will hate the rest of her life.* Turn: from the bandstand the park band plays; Helena hears, in her head, the violin piece the child cannot finish, and agrees.

**5. The Card Index** — Fri 28 May 1943 — Friedrich & Klara.
Skaryszewska again: the office, the card index, the quotas, Bierbaum's appetite for commissions; the racial office's clerk; the machinery in action — the child moved to a "holding list," washed, fed, put to sorting rags in the labour office basement; Klara's first complicity; Böhm's price for a "family exemption"; Generalmajor Ritter's warning about political cleanliness. Turn: Friedrich finds his own signature on another child's transport list and reads the destination.

**6. The Courthouse** — Early June 1943 — Wanda (& Marysia).
The burnt ghetto's edge; the Grodzki courthouse at Leszno with its two doors; the survivors still being pulled out of the ruins; Wanda's work; the sewer boys; Marysia's first errand when her mother sends her with a message and does not say what it is; the dead city; the szmalcownik Wacek on the corner. Turn: Wacek takes Marysia's face into his memory and writes it down.

**7. The Photograph** — Mid-June 1943 — Helena.
Dr Teichmann's assessment room: the calipers, the photographs, the mother asked to undress, the "family history" sheet; Friedrich forced to stand by the window; Ilse von Brandt's petition surfaces; Helena's revulsion; the bargain she is asked to sign. Turn: on the way home a black car follows her, stops, and Dörffel asks her, politely, what business an unmarried Polish music teacher has with a Prussian major.

**8. The Dead City** — Mid-July 1943 — Friedrich.
Ordered to reconnoitre the ruins for quartering "clearance labourers": the ghetto's ashes, the Gęsiówka, the smell, the man in the cellar; Friedrich's one act of mercy (and the fear that follows it); his letter to Erika, dropped in the fire; the ruin of his ideals laid out like rubble. Turn: back in Warsaw he learns the child has been moved a third time — by the Gestapo, on Dörffel's order.

## PART TWO — THE SHORTENING ROPE (Sept 1943–July 1944)

**9. The Notices** — October 1943 — Helena & Tomek.
The public execution notices pasted on the walls; the growing terror; Tomek's work; Helena chosen by the underground to be the point of contact with the Major; her first drop; the death of the boy who made her a courier; the beginnings of her own war. Turn: Helena keeps a German deserter's blood on her coat and does not wash it off for a week.

**10. The House of Wolves** — Christmas 1943 — Ilse.
Falkenhorst: the family at the table, the wine, the talk of the East, the child question raised over dessert, the photo of Marysia passed from hand to hand, Grete's husband explaining the race office's work as a farmer explains a harrow; Ilse's dead husband's boots by the door; her want. Turn: Ilse writes to Posen, and the letter has a name in it.

**11. The Tram at Szucha** — December 1943 — Helena.
The night raid; the arrest (Wacek's price); the cell called the tram; the sessions; the questions about a German officer; the woman in the cell who knows Marysia; the Pawiak van; the Patronat woman; Dörffel's patience; the child's name produced from a folder. Turn: Helena understands that the Gestapo was not hunting her — it was hunting Friedrich, and she is the door.

**12. The Girl in the Laundry** — January 1944 — Marysia.
The child's own chapter: the basement of the labour office, the rag-sorting, the hunger, the other children, the small Germanized girl called Elschen who no longer remembers her Polish name, Klara's sandwiches, the rats, the songs; and the day the tall major comes and talks to her about music. Turn: she calls him Papa by accident, and he does not deny it, and she has never seen a man's face do that.

**13. The Exchange** — February 1944 — Friedrich.
Kutschera dead in the street; the reprisals and the wall; the underground's demand, carried by a stranger in a church; the price asked of Friedrich (information, and later something worse); Tomek's arrest; the list of hostages three hundred names long; the signature Friedrich can and cannot put to it. Turn: Tomek's name read out at the wall; Helena's hands.

**14. The Fever** — March 1944 — Helena.
Typhus in Pawiak; the infirmary; the Patronat's work; Friedrich's money and Kellner's courage and Klara's forged release; freedom bought at four prices; the mother comes out alive and emptied; the day she learns the child's transfer order has already gone to the transport officer. Turn: the order is signed — and the signature is not Friedrich's.

**15. The Loading Ramp** — April 1944 — Klara & Friedrich.
The Ostbahn sidings; the children's transport; the Germanization families waiting at the far end of the platform; Elschen's new mother with her hat and her basket; the small suitcases with the names chalked on them; Friedrich's attempt to stop one name; the SS's counter-move; his public humiliation and his demotion. Turn: Friedrich takes the child off the train and puts her in the hands of the nuns, which is a desertion.

**16. The Nuns' House** — May 1944 — Marysia.
Nowogrodzka: the convent, the hidden children, the lessons, the silence, the priest, the boy in the next room who cannot remember his name either; Marysia begins to keep a ledger of names, a child's resistance; the night of the search. Turn: she writes her mother's name in the ledger, and under it, in the space the nun said to leave empty, a second name she does not understand why she knows.

**17. The Tin Box** — June 1944 — Helena & Friedrich.
The escape of the child to the countryside arranged at last; Helena's father's tin box opened for the first time: the three unopened letters; the reckoning of ten years; the last night the two of them are alone; the tenderness and the grief of it; the terms of a future that will not happen. Turn: at dawn, guns in the east that are not German.

**18. The Twentieth of July** — 20 July 1944 – 1 August 1944 — Friedrich.
The bomb in the Führer's hut; the purge; Friedrich's cousin on the list; his own name in another man's notebook; the cellars; the warning he sends to Hoża by way of Kellner; the last look at his daughter across a courtyard; the order to report. Turn: at five o'clock the first shots on the avenue, and the city is rising.

## PART THREE — THE FIRE (August 1944–January 1945)

**19. W Hour** — Aug 1–4, 1944 — Helena & Marysia.
The barricades, the flag, the unbelievable joy; the field hospital in the cellars off Freta; the first wounded; the children's ward; the German counterattack; the flamethrowers in the Old Town. Turn: the first tank at the barricade, and the smell of burning, and the order that no one will now be taken alive.

**20. Black Saturday** — Aug 5–12, 1944 — Helena.
Wola burning; the Dirlewanger men; the hospital; the shot in the street; the German boy soldier Helena saves and the Polish boy she cannot; the buckets of blood; the doctor's last cigarette; the column of survivors with their hands up and the guns at their backs. Turn: Helena is put against the wall — and a familiar voice, from behind a staff car, tells her to get into the lorry.

**21. The Sewers** — Aug 20 – Sept 2, 1944 — Marysia.
The Old Town's last cellar; the collapse; the walk into the dark under the streets; the water to the chin; the babies's heads kept above water; the stranger with the rope who carries children; the light at the manhole in Żoliborz; the city on fire behind them. Turn: on the other side, a man's voice calls her by the name only two people in the world used.

**22. The Iron Rain** — Sept–Oct 1944 — Friedrich.
The last days of the German Warsaw: the burning library, the dynamited house, the men who obey and the man who refuses; the deserters' cellar; the meeting with the SS patrol; the order to put the city on the trains; his own name on a list. Turn: 2 October, the white flag, the columns, the ruins; and Friedrich walking, anonymous, into the evacuation.

**23. Pruszków** — October–November 1944 — Helena.
Dulag 121: the sheds, the selections, the roar of the loudspeakers; the mother who has nothing left to sell; the children's transport lists; Helena gives her daughter a new name; the barbed wire; the train, the slats, the face at the gap; the whistle. Turn: the train with Marysia in it pulls out while Helena is on her knees in the grass, holding a paper with a wrong name on it, and the paper is the last thing she has of her.

**24. Cold Christmas** — Dec 1944 – 17 Jan 1945 — Friedrich.
The hunt; Ilse's letter with a bargain; the price of a place on a lorry out of the Reich; the last interview with Dörffel; Friedrich takes the child from the house on Nowogrodzka and walks west through the snow; the checkpoint; the shot. Turn: the Red Army's guns on the Vistula; a man in a hole in the ground with a child, and a choice he makes with his body.

**25. The Snow** — Jan–Feb 1945 — Helena & Kellner.
The crossing of the frozen plain; the refugee road; the dead horses; the woman who gives a stranger milk; the farmhouse; the child alive in a stranger's bed; the two halves of the photograph made whole; the burial in the frozen ground with a Polish priest and a German prayer; the mother's decision about what will be told and what will never be told. Turn: the last image of the war — the wall still standing, the city gone, and the door in the wall.

## CODA

**Epilogue: The Gardener's Daughter** — 1946 / 1963 / 1989 — Helena (1946), Marysia (1963), Anna (1989).
Warsaw in ruins and rising; the violin that came back; the ledger of names; Marysia grown, a doctor, her mother's lie discovered; the two halves of the photograph reunited on a kitchen table in 1963; and in 1989 a granddaughter at a window in Warsaw, with a train ticket in her pocket and both halves of the picture in an envelope, about to walk out of a life that was built for her by other people's mercies. Final page returns to Hoża Street and the house that is still standing, and the caretaker with the keys.
