# THE PRICE OF MERCY — Story Bible (private working notes)

**Working title:** *The Price of Mercy*
**Form:** Historical novel. 25 chapters + epilogue. Target 5,000–6,000 words per chapter (~140,000 words total).
**Setting:** Warsaw (chiefly), with the Oder/Mark Brandenburg estate, Munich 1933, and post-war Warsaw.
**Span:** May 1943 – January 1945, with a 1933 backstory and an epilogue that runs forward.

---

## 1. The two leads

**Helena Wojtera**, 33 in 1943. Born Warsaw 1910, daughter of Stanisław Wojtera, a railway engineer who died in 1939 (heart, during the siege — or in a reprisal; keep simple: died Sept 1939 of a heart attack while helping dig trenches; her mother Zofia? no, mother died 1938 of TB). A violinist: scholarship to the Munich conservatory 1931–33. Back in Warsaw she teaches violin and piano, does piecework, and speaks excellent German — a fact she keeps quiet. Very proud, physically brave, a liar only for her daughter. Never married. Told everyone the child's father was a Polish fiancé, "Adam," who died in 1933. Keeps one half of a photograph in the lining of her winter coat.

**Major Friedrich von Brandt**, 31 in 1943. Born 1912 on Gut Falkenhorst near Küstrin on the Oder: a Prussian Junker family, Protestant, land and army. Commissioned 1933 into a cavalry regiment; general staff training; Poland 1939, France 1940. On the staff of the German commander's office in Warsaw from 1942, responsible for billets, labour allocations and Wehrmacht workshops — a desk officer with a roving commission, which gives him legal access to Warsaw's labour machinery. Married 1937 (arranged, by his father) to **Erika née von Wense**, 32. Their son **Konrad** was born 1938 and died of meningitis in 1941. He is a decent man who has been mediocre at the one moment that mattered. His half of the photograph is in his pay book.

## 2. The core secret and its mathematics

- Munich, spring 1933. Helena, 22, a scholarship violinist; Friedrich, 20, a young officer posted to Munich. Engaged in March 1933. His family — above all his father, **Generalmajor a.D. Ewald von Brandt** — destroyed it in May 1933: police pressure (Helena was a Polish national, arrestable and deportable), a signed renunciation obtained from her, a lie told to Friedrich. She was pregnant and did not know it yet when she left, on the 6.20 a.m. train, and told him nothing.
- Marysia (Maria Anna Wojtera) born 3 December 1933 in Warsaw. Nine years old in May 1943. Gifted violinist, brave, quick, dark-haired, tall for her age.
- The documents that matter: the signed renunciation (in the family's papers); three letters Friedrich wrote to Warsaw that Helena's father returned unopened (they survive in a tin box of her father's things); the half photographs.
- What no one but the readers and the leads may ever know — and what each of them will have to decide about — is the child's paternity. In German-occupied Warsaw, naming the father destroys the father (racial defilement; court-martial) and hands the child to the Reich; not naming him sends the child to labour, to a camp, or to the Germanization machinery. That dilemma is the engine of the book.

## 3. The mechanism (the plot machine)

- **The łapanka** takes Helena and Marysia (Ch. 1). Friedrich sees them across the yard of the transit camp at Skaryszewska 8 and hides behind the paperwork: the child must not be sent east, so she is moved off the Reich transport list — onto the list of the racial office as a candidate for *Eindeutschung* (Germanization).
- **The price:** to keep her in Warsaw, alive, the mother must let her daughter be processed as "a child of German blood." To end the processing, the mother must name the father — which destroys him and does not get the child back. Every escape from the machine is bought with a worse coin.
- **The counter-machinery:** the Warsaw underworld of rescue — the welfare office, the underground, the courthouse at Leszno (before the ghetto's destruction) as a way through; the nuns on Nowogrodzka who hide Jewish children; the Patronat at Pawiak; the blackmailers; the informers.
- **The rival claimants:** Friedrich's widowed sister-in-law **Ilse von Brandt**, 38, in Posen, childless, who petitioned the racial office for a "German-blooded child" long before she knew whose child; and **SS-Hauptsturmführer Werner Dörffel** at Szucha, who notices the anomaly in a Major's paperwork and who enjoys pulling the thread.

## 4. Cast

**Polish / Warsaw**
- **Zofia Mrożek**, 61 — caretaker of the house at Hoża 14, Helena's landlady and second mother; knows every secret on the street and keeps all of them.
- **Tomasz "Tomek" Mrożek**, 24 — Zofia's son; tram conductor; Home Army courier; in love with Helena; the book's best young man, and its one unforgivable loss.
- **Dr Antoni Kwiatkowski**, 55 — physician at the Praga hospital; false papers; a gambler's nerve.
- **Wanda Ostrowska**, 41 — social welfare worker, Żegota, leads children out of the ghetto and to hiding; the book's conscience.
- **Rachel Perel**, 8 — Jewish child hidden in the coal cellar at Hoża 14; Polish cover name Renia Zielińska. Marysia's secret sister.
- **Pani Halina Rowińska**, 44 — neighbour; salońskie gossip, small mercies, a betrayal she does not intend; useful as a mirror of ordinary Warsaw.
- **Wacek Górski**, 33 — blackmailer (*szmalcownik*); he sells names to Szucha and lives at the Rialto.
- **Stefan "Horpyna"** — the smuggler of the courtyards, 50, mule-driver, informer's enemy, saved by Helena in Chapter 6.

**German**
- **Generalmajor Wolfram Ritter**, 56 — Kommandant of the Wehrmacht office in Warsaw. Old school, tired, keeps clean hands by never touching anything.
- **Leutnant Klaus Adler**, 23 — Friedrich's assistant; Hitler Youth to Russia to Warsaw; eager; the future.
- **Oberzahlmeister Bernhard Wollweber**, 51 — requisitions and money, useful and corruptible.
- **Klara Seidler**, 28 — German secretary in the labour office; from Kassel; a civil servant's daughter who learns what her signature does.
- **Herr Albert Böhm**, 47 — Arbeitsamt official who sells exemptions for zloty and gold.
- **SS-Hauptsturmführer Werner Dörffel**, 36 — Szucha, Department IV; lawyer; patient; the antagonist who is never cruel in a hurry.
- **SS-Untersturmführer Oskar Bierbaum**, 29 — Skaryszewska and later the transports; killable by his own vanity.
- **Dr Alfred Teichmann**, 44 — racial examiner (Rasse- und Siedlungswesen), from RuSHA, quiet, uses calipers, believes his sheets.
- **Erika von Brandt**, 32 — Friedrich's wife in Falkenhorst; numb; keeps her dead son's room dusted.
- **Ilse von Brandt**, 38 — Friedrich's sister-in-law in Posen (Warthegau); the dead Konrad's widow; wants a daughter.
- **Margarete "Grete" Krämer née von Brandt**, 34 — Friedrich's sister, married to **SS-Sturmbannführer Hermann Krämer**, 45, of the Rasse- und Siedlungswesen in Posen. Faith.
- **Gefreiter Bruno Kellner**, 41 — Berlin grocer; Friedrich's driver and the book's small good German; gives false names; carries the child in Chapter 25.

**Historical figures** appear only as background weather (Fischer, Hahn, Kutschera, Himmler, Frank, Stroop) — named sparingly and accurately.

## 5. Rules of the book

1. Third person, past tense, close on one POV per scene; chapters may carry two or three POVs. POV characters: Helena, Friedrich, Marysia, Tomek, Wanda, Klara, Ilse, Dörffel (once), Kellner (once).
2. Each chapter has at least one piece of the real machinery of the occupation inside it: the ration, the notice, the register, the transport, the assessment, the ransom, the wall — and one act of mercy with a visible price.
3. Period-frank: violence and sensuality are written boldly, but the camera is a witness, not a customer. Sexual violence is never depicted for atmosphere.
4. Polish and German phrases in italics; the idiom of the street. No lecturing the reader; the machinery is shown in use.
5. Every chapter ends on a turn — an arrival, a door, a paper, a gun, a face.
6. The mercy loop must pay: Helena hides Rachel; Friedrich saves a stranger in the ruins; someone saves Marysia at the rails; the mercies bought in hell are all somebody's unreturned loan.

## 6. Continuity ledger (update as drafted)

- **Munich 1933:** Friedrich's 20th birthday, Sunday 12 March 1933 = the day in the Englischer Garten pavilion by the lake; a band playing beyond the trees. Helena left Munich 6 June 1933 (her uncle Stanisław Bronarski's flat; her uncle's residence permit was used as leverage). She signed a dictated renunciation on 30 May 1933 in Generalmajor Ewald von Brandt's study at Falkenhorst. She posted a four-line letter to Friedrich on 22 June 1933 (received Munich 1 July) saying she had accepted the settlement. She did not know she was pregnant; she learned it in Warsaw in July 1933. She never cashed the 500-mark draft (it is still in the tin box, uncashed, sent back once and returned again).
- **Friedrich:** got 36 hours' leave Sept 1933, stood in the Hoża 14 courtyard in the rain, did not go up, went back to Munich. Married Erika von Wense, summer 1937. Son Konrad b. 1938, d. Feb 1941 (meningitis). His half of the photo-strip is in his pay book. Wedding ring removed at some point; white mark on fourth finger.
- **The photo strip:** four frames from an Automat on Sendlinger Straße, autumn 1932; cut in half with scissors in the pavilion in March 1933. Helena's half is sewn into the lining of her winter coat (left breast). Friedrich's half is in his Soldbuch.
- **Objects:** Helena's father's Błonie nickel pocket watch (pawned 22 May 1943 for 110 zł); her mother's gold ring (kept, in the coat hem); her father's violin (a Hopf, 1790s, kept, unsold); Marysia's ¾ violin with its half-size bow (in the pawnshop on Chmielna, recovered 1941). The tin box: under the fourth floorboard at the alcove wall of Helena's room at Hoża 14. Contents: Munich conservatory certificate, Residenz recital programme Dec 1932, dried rose from the Nymphenburger Straße, three returned letters, the uncashed draft.
- **Skaryszewska:** Bierbaum (SS-Untersturmführer) commandant; detail three = children's rag-sorting in the school basement; a nurse in the camp infirmary who certifies people unfit for transport (Tomek's contact); camp hospital link = Jagiellońska Street hospital.
- **Warsaw prices, May 1943:** legal bread ration 200g/day; black-market bread 50 zł/kg; labour exemption with signature ("Familienausweis") 4,000–6,000 zł; false baptismal certificates from Tomek's printer 2,000 zł; milk from the Zamość children's fund depot via Wanda's Wednesday list.
- **Names:** Zofia Mrożek (caretaker, 61) and her son Tomasz "Tomek" Mrożek (24, tram conductor, AK courier, ex-cello pupil). Pani Halina Rowińska (neighbour, gossip). Wanda Ostrowska (RGO welfare, Żegota-adjacent, works from the office on Marszałkowska; Wednesdays). Rachel Perel "Renia Zielińska" (8, in the coal cellar at Hoża 14 since February 1943). Wacek Górski (the man from the pawnshop, chin in his collar — szmalcownik). Klara Seidler (Friedrich's secretary, 28, Kassel). Oberzahlmeister Bernhard Wollweber. Gefreiter Bruno Kellner (driver, 41, Berlin grocer, "weak heart" certificate). Leutnant Klaus Adler (23, Friedrich's assistant).
- **Ch1–3 established:** Friedrich's office = 2nd floor, commandant's building, Nowy Świat; his section = Referat für Arbeitseinsatz und Quartierwesen; Helena's labour card = Wehrmacht hospital laundry, Wiejska Street, 6–6; Helena was released Sat 22 May, the child retained in detail three; Leutnant Horst Peltz (7th Cavalry) buried Fri 21 May at the Evangelical graveyard on Młynarska; the KdS file number on the child's card; Dörffel's office telephoned at 15.20 on 21 May asking about the Wojtera file.

## 7. Continuity ledger, Part Two onward

- **Autumn 1943 (ch9):** 16 Oct 1943 — first public executions by hanging in Warsaw streets; Kuba (Henryk Zaremba, 17) hanged; Helena sworn in as a courier in Sept 1943 and taught by Kuba (rules: nothing worth more than a lesson; never the same route; if stopped, be stupid, not brave — "show them the violin"). Tomek reveals that "the Loaf" (the German who sold the underground a list in March 1943) is Major von Brandt. Erich (?), a 19-year-old Wehrmacht deserter from Gelsenkirchen ("Ich hab nicht geschossen"), sheltered on Świętokrzyska, operated on by Dr Antoni Kwiatkowski. The coat: blood seeped through the lining; permanent brown stain across the lower corner of the photograph.
- **Christmas 1943, Falkenhorst (ch10):** Generalmajor a.D. Ewald von Brandt, 73. Friedrich's brother Konrad (the elder, "the one with the laugh") married Ilse 1935, killed in Russia; telegram 20 Sept 1941. Ilse's application 9 Sept 1941; on 8 June 1943 she saw Marysia's photograph in Posen; in Oct 1943 she read at the Institute, in Warsaw, the copy of Stanisław Wojtera's letter of 1933 (Security Service Munich copy). Grete (Margarete Krämer née von Brandt) and her husband SS-Sturmbannführer Hermann Krämer (Rasse- und Siedlungswesen, Posen), children Wolf (9) and Dorle (6). Erika keeps the nursery of the dead son Konrad (d. Feb 1941, meningitis). On 27 Dec 1943 Ilse wrote to the Rasse- und Siedlungsamt Posen asking for a speedy finding, an entry of the father's name without the mother's knowledge, and placement in the Reich before the mother could contest; Krämer wrote separately for the file report.
- **January 1944 (ch11):** Arrested at dawn 6 Jan 1944; the Polish plainclothesman (gold filling) tells her Wacek sold her name for 700 zł; Wacek lives third floor at the Rialto. Held Szucha (calls "trams"), cellmate Krysia Zaremba; the scout girl dies at the wall. Dörffel's offer: a signed statement naming Friedrich as the father (plus Lieutnant's name, plus money) in exchange for the child's transfer to the Zentrale für Kinderfürsorge. Transferred to Pawiak (Dzielna) on 10 Jan, wing "Serbia", category *in Untersuchung*. Last image: she is a door, not a prisoner.
- **January 1944, Ostbahn depot, Praga (ch12):** Labour-reserve detail of 22 children; foreman Herr Bader; guard Didko; Irena (14, cough), Józek, Bronka (lost three fingertips at the press). Letters: the safe German formula; the mother's letters signed "Deine Mutter", one written in a hospital in Dec. Klara Seidler's Tuesday visits: the mother is in Pawiak; 29 children of Skaryszewska went to Łódź in November; a parcel of thread/pencils/soap at the gate on Sundays. Friday inspection: Friedrich visits the shed; Marysia says "Papa" without meaning to (Adler hears the sound and will use it in March); she begins a hidden list of names in the mortar of her bunk, ending with "Me. Maria Anna Wojtera… who is alive."
